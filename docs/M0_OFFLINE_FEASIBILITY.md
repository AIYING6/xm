# M0 — Offline Feasibility Audit

## Protocol

Artifact: `artifacts/m0/m0_offline_feasibility.json` generated by `scripts/run_m0_offline_feasibility.py`.

- zero training; zero environment rollout; zero new evaluation tape;
- five frozen 1M T1 UTR final checkpoints (seeds 2201–2205);
- 24 recorded, actor-legal F0 pre-trigger states per seed;
- all probes are in-memory and restore parameters before proceeding;
- these diagnostics are exploratory only and are **not** return estimates, a new selection threshold, or an efficacy claim.

## Topology sensitivity probe

On the same recorded pre-trigger state, only non-self messages *from* relay node 1 were removed; all other legal edges, including the legal Scout→Attacker direct path, were retained. The mean action-distribution KL values were:

| UTR seed | local relay-deletion policy KL |
|---:|---:|
| 2201 | 0.00349 |
| 2202 | 0.01420 |
| 2203 | 0.00327 |
| 2204 | 0.00087 |
| 2205 | 0.00620 |

The strongest recorded UTR seed (2202, by F0/OOD return) has the **largest** local deletion response, while a weak seed (2204) has the smallest. This is a direct contradiction of the simple premise “weaker policies fail because they are too graph-sensitive.” Consequently, a spectral/Lipschitz graph-smoothing method is rejected as the primary method. It would be scientifically unsafe to impose smoothness merely because the deployment perturbation is topological.

## Behavioural parameter-sensitivity probe

The same state set was evaluated after an in-memory, deterministic, norm-bounded actor-parameter perturbation of radius 0.01. Mean policy KL values were small and narrow:

| UTR seed | parameter-perturbation policy KL |
|---:|---:|
| 2201 | 1.95e-05 |
| 2202 | 1.91e-05 |
| 2203 | 1.76e-05 |
| 2204 | 1.96e-05 |
| 2205 | 1.75e-05 |

There is no clean strong-versus-weak separation. This does **not** falsify flatness-aware optimisation: the probe is a fixed-state behavioural proxy, not PPO-loss sharpness across the training distribution. It does mean the candidate must be motivated by mature generalisation/stability theory plus the fixed topology-OOD setting, not by a false claim that T1 already proves a sharpness mechanism.

## Operator-norm audit

The two GAT projection spectral norms vary across seeds (e.g., `gat1.proj` 1.26–2.45; `gat2.proj` 1.08–1.79), but neither their ordering nor the local relay-deletion KL ordering separates good and weak policies. Spectral normalisation therefore has no positive local signal and a clear mechanistic contradiction for its naive rationale.

## Feasibility verdict

| Candidate | Offline feasibility | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| TC-SAM-UTR | No contradiction; policy-optimisation precedent; requires no actor-boundary change. | **Viable** |
| Spectral/Lipschitz graph policy | Local evidence contradicts the presumed excess-sensitivity failure mechanism. | **Reject** |
| Local adversarial graph augmentation | No offline evidence of a separate gap beyond UTR; high risk of illegal/ambiguous perturbations and adaptive feedback. | **Reject** |
| Graph information bottleneck | Literature-valid but incompatible with the closed support-utilisation route and not a small matched-SG adaptation. | **Reject** |

The audit intentionally does not demand a perfect predictive law across all seeds. It does reject premises that are directly reversed by the recorded evidence.
