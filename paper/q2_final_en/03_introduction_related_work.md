# 1. Introduction

Heterogeneous UAV teams often combine sensing, communication, and task-execution roles. In such systems, cooperative capability depends not only on each vehicle's kinematics but also on whether task-relevant information can traverse legal sensing and communication relations. Graph-based multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has provided useful representations for structured interaction [2,3], and graph neural policies have been applied to multi-UAV search, tracking, and swarm control [6,7]. However, a relay-node failure changes the available relation structure itself. When the failed relay removes a multi-hop route but a direct Scout--Attacker route remains legal, the resulting challenge is not complete information loss. It is a topology/path and task-support reconfiguration problem under a strict decentralized information boundary.

Existing robust MARL methods study sensitivity to partner changes, model uncertainty, or adversarial variation [4], while distributionally robust reinforcement learning studies decision making under distribution shifts [5]. Related ideas also appear in group-robust learning, active domain randomization, and curriculum design [11--13]. These lines motivate the question of how a training distribution should expose a policy to difficult conditions. They do not, by themselves, establish a method that is fair or directly transferable to the frozen relay-failure contract studied here: the present task fixes heterogeneous roles, legal communication semantics, failure onset and duration, reward, and actor information. Nor do they imply that a heuristic reweighting rule has a general distributionally robust optimization guarantee.

We therefore ask a deliberately controlled question. Given the same Single-Graph MAPPO backbone, PPO objective, reward, topology-perturbation support, nominal-condition exposure, training budget, and paired evaluation protocol, does bounded adaptive reweighting of six predefined relay-failure groups improve mission performance relative to uniform reweighting? The question has two parts. First, do gains appear on canonical F0 and on mean and minimum mission score across the frozen perturbation family? Second, are those gains reliable across independently completed training cohorts? The latter condition is part of the scientific question rather than a post hoc caveat, because adaptive training feedback can be training-seed sensitive.

To answer these questions, we introduce bounded adaptive topology-perturbation reweighting Single-Graph MAPPO (DRTP-SG-MAPPO; DRTP). DRTP retains a fixed 50% nominal-condition anchor and redistributes the remaining failure mass across six predefined groups using group return relative to nominal competence, exponential reweighting, smoothing, and bounded-simplex projection. Its primary comparator, Uniform Topology Randomization Single-Graph MAPPO (UTR-SG-MAPPO; UTR), has the same 116,728-parameter actor--critic, PPO settings, environment, reward, perturbation support, budget, and execution-time information boundary; it differs only by keeping the six conditional group weights uniform. The formal comparison consequently identifies an empirical difference between bounded adaptive and uniform reweighting in this task. It does not establish that online adaptation is necessary relative to every fixed non-uniform distribution.

Our contributions are as follows.

1. We formulate a relay-node-failure UAV coordination task in which failure removes the Relay's sensing and communication relations while preserving a physically legal direct Scout--Attacker path when available. The task isolates legal topology/path and task-support reconfiguration without injecting failure labels or global topology truth into the decentralized actor.
2. We define DRTP as a bounded training-time reweighting controller over six frozen failure/topology groups. It changes neither the actor--critic architecture, PPO objective, reward, nor execution-time information, which concentrates the method contrast on the training distribution.
3. We conduct a prospective, parameter- and exposure-matched five-seed 10M comparison with a single final-checkpoint rule, a pre-frozen paired evaluation tape, seed-level paired reporting, condition-level endpoints, safety outcomes, and risk-set trigger-validity checks. The formal cohort favors DRTP on F0, `J_pert,mean`, and `J_pert,worst`, while preserving a timeout--collision trade-off rather than claiming uniform safety improvement.
4. We retain reliability evidence as part of the result boundary. A separately completed three-method cohort reverses the DRTP--UTR direction on both evaluation tapes. Thus, the paper reports formal-cohort upside together with training-cohort sensitivity instead of claiming stable superiority or strict OOD generalization.

# 3. Related work and positioning

## 3.1 Graph-structured multi-agent learning

Graph attention permits a policy to aggregate neighbor information according to a graph and learned attention weights [2], while topology-aware cooperative policy-gradient work has shown that agent topology can matter for multi-agent optimization [3]. Graph representations have also been used for UAV cooperative search, tracking, and large-swarm control [6,7]. This literature motivates the shared graph encoder in our task, but graph encoding itself is not the contribution of DRTP. We keep the Single-Graph actor--critic fixed across UTR and DRTP and instead study how the distribution of topology perturbations is sampled during training.

## 3.2 Robust learning and adaptive training distributions

Robust MARL includes minimax formulations for partner or environmental variation [4], and distributionally robust Q-learning addresses ambiguity in a transition or environment distribution [5]. Group-robust reweighting [11], active domain randomization [12], and reinforcement-learning curricula [13] further motivate adapting exposure to groups or task parameters during training. DRTP inherits only the empirical intuition that a fixed training distribution may underemphasize currently difficult conditions. It is not a minimax solver, does not optimize a certified group-DRO objective, and does not claim a worst-case guarantee. Its contribution is a reproducible, bounded six-group sampler embedded in a fixed UAV relay-failure contract and evaluated against a matched uniform sampler.

## 3.3 Communication-limited UAV cooperation and fault-related topology changes

MARL has been applied to UAV swarm communication under jamming [8] and to multi-hop UAV relay communication with trajectory and power decisions [9]. Communication-network studies also describe how link or node failures can require path or topology reconfiguration [15,16]. These studies establish application relevance, but their action spaces, communication objectives, learning algorithms, reward definitions, and information boundaries differ from the present relay-failure coordination task. They are therefore used for problem positioning rather than converted into nominally fair performance baselines.

## 3.4 Comparator boundary

The paper's primary causal comparison is UTR versus DRTP under a common SG-MAPPO backbone. MAPPO-NoGraph is reported as an external performance reference because it shares the task, seeds, budget, and evaluation tape but differs in graph-message input and parameter count. The independent cohort additionally includes a fixed non-uniform sampler, yet it has a separate cohort and evaluation tape and does not reproduce the formal DRTP direction. Neither comparison can be backfilled as evidence that online adaptation is necessary relative to every static sampler. This evidence hierarchy separates the controlled within-contract claim from external performance context and from the reliability limitation.
