# Abstract

Relay-node failures in heterogeneous unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) teams can reconfigure legal communication paths and task-support relations without necessarily causing complete information loss. We study whether training-time control of this structural perturbation distribution improves cooperative performance under such failures. We introduce bounded adaptive topology-perturbation reweighting Single-Graph multi-agent proximal policy optimization (DRTP-SG-MAPPO; DRTP), which preserves the policy backbone, proximal policy optimization objective, reward, execution-time information boundary, and a 50% nominal-condition anchor, while adaptively reweighting six predefined failure/topology groups. Its primary comparator, Uniform Topology Randomization Single-Graph MAPPO (UTR-SG-MAPPO; UTR), has the same 116,728-parameter backbone, perturbation support, training budget, and paired evaluation protocol.

In a prospective formal cohort of five paired training seeds trained for 10M environment steps, DRTP outperformed UTR on the canonical relay-failure condition, the mean across ten frozen perturbation conditions, and the minimum over those conditions: the mean paired differences were +52.13, +55.00, and +63.01 mission-score units, respectively, and all five seeds were positive on each of these three endpoints. DRTP also reduced the mean timeout rate from 0.874 to 0.694, while increasing the collision rate from 0.005 to 0.008, indicating a task--safety trade-off rather than uniform safety improvement. However, a separate independent three-method training cohort did not reproduce the DRTP direction, showed negative paired task endpoints, and contained catastrophic training-seed outcomes. Cross-evaluation-tape diagnostics and a post hoc evaluation on six training-excluded onset--duration tuples preserved this cohort-level direction difference.

DRTP therefore provides formal-cohort evidence that bounded adaptive perturbation reweighting can improve relay-failure task performance relative to matched uniform weighting, but its benefit is not established as stable across training cohorts. The results motivate reliability-aware reporting for topology-adaptive multi-agent training rather than a claim of universal robustness or strict out-of-distribution generalization.
