# Abstract

Relay-node failures in heterogeneous unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) teams can reconfigure legal communication paths and task-support relations without necessarily causing complete information loss. We study this controlled coordination problem with bounded adaptive topology-perturbation reweighting Single-Graph multi-agent proximal policy optimization (DRTP-SG-MAPPO; DRTP). DRTP preserves the policy backbone, PPO objective, reward, execution-time information boundary, and a 50% nominal-condition anchor, and adaptively reweights six predefined failure/topology groups. Its primary comparator, Uniform Topology Randomization Single-Graph MAPPO (UTR-SG-MAPPO; UTR), has the same 116,728-parameter backbone, perturbation support, training budget, and paired evaluation protocol.

In a prospective formal cohort of five paired training seeds trained for 10M environment steps, DRTP exceeded UTR on canonical relay failure, the mean across ten frozen perturbation conditions, and the minimum over those conditions. Mean paired differences were +52.13, +55.00, and +63.01 mission-score units, respectively, and all five seeds were positive on each robustness endpoint. DRTP reduced mean timeout from 0.874 to 0.694, while collision increased from 0.005 to 0.008. A separate independent three-method cohort, however, reversed the DRTP--UTR direction and contained a catastrophic DRTP seed. Cross-tape and post hoc training-excluded-condition evaluations retained this cohort-level contrast.

DRTP therefore provides formal-cohort evidence that bounded adaptive reweighting can improve relay-failure task performance relative to matched uniform weighting. Its benefit is not established as reliable across training cohorts; the study reports this limitation rather than claiming universal robustness or strict out-of-distribution generalization.
