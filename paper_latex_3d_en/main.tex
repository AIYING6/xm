\begin{abstract}
Heterogeneous UAV teams must close cooperative kill chains under incomplete sensing and limited, degraded communication. Even when a multi-agent policy eventually succeeds, it may take many steps to re-establish effective coordination after a node or link failure. This paper therefore focuses on accelerating post-failure coordination recovery while retaining near-saturated task reliability, rather than only maximizing the terminal success rate. We propose EA-RG-MAPPO-S, a task-graph-driven multi-relation coordination method for 3DOF heterogeneous UAV interception. The method encodes task-dependency, communication availability, and state-dependent relations into a multi-relation graph processed by state-dependent, edge-feature-modulated attention under centralized training and decentralized execution; a task-support relation acts as a task-dependent relational mask over delivered communication, and a structured role-pair initialization prior (Gate Prior) stabilizes role-structured optimization. On a locked three-seed held-out evaluation (10{,}800 episodes), EA-RG achieves a post-failure recovery rate of $0.971 \pm 0.021$ with a recovery time of $10.8 \pm 0.6$ steps, reducing recovery latency by approximately 38\%, 34\%, and 59\% relative to MAPPO, HAPPO, and a wider single-graph baseline, respectively, while maintaining near-saturated task reliability (HAPPO and the wider single-graph baseline obtain slightly higher terminal recovery rates). Mechanistic analysis further indicates that the Gate Prior primarily acts as a structured optimization prior that improves cross-seed consistency rather than as a runtime adaptive mechanism. These results show that task-graph relational coordination can substantially accelerate post-failure recovery without requiring superior terminal reliability on every metric; EA-RG trades additional computational cost for faster task-level recovery.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} task graph; multi-relation coordination; post-failure recovery; heterogeneous UAV cooperative interception; multi-agent reinforcement learning; limited communication; intermittent sensing
