\title{Task-Graph-Driven Multi-Relation Coordination for Fast Post-Failure Recovery in Heterogeneous UAV Cooperative Interception}

\begin{abstract}
Heterogeneous UAV teams operating under incomplete sensing and degraded communication may lose task-chain functionality after a critical node failure. This work therefore studies post-failure recovery dynamics rather than terminal task success alone. We propose EA-RG-MAPPO-S, a task-graph-driven multi-relation coordination method for 3DOF heterogeneous UAV cooperative interception. The method represents perception, communication, and task-dependent support relations in a multi-relation graph and processes them using state-dependent, edge-feature-modulated attention under centralized training and decentralized execution. A structured Gate Prior is further introduced to improve the optimization stability of role-pair modulation. On a locked three-seed held-out evaluation comprising 10{,}800 episodes, EA-RG achieves a post-failure recovery rate of $0.971 \pm 0.021$; censor-aware survival analysis shows that its advantage is concentrated in early recovery: at $\tau = 80$, corresponding to the active node-failure interval, EA-RG reduces restricted mean survival time from $15.51$ steps for MAPPO to $11.81$ steps, with all three training seeds in the same direction, while full-horizon recovery remains competitive rather than uniformly superior. Ablation and mechanism analyses further show that the Gate Prior improves optimization stability, Task-Support provides an empirical relational benefit, whereas static Role-Pair Modulation has limited independent benefit. A pre-specified zero-shot out-of-distribution evaluation (8400 episodes) then delimits the boundary of this advantage: it is partially preserved under geometry shifts, reversed under communication-topology shifts, and overwhelmed by maneuver shifts (Gate~C). These results indicate that task-graph multi-relation coordination improves the temporal concentration of early post-failure recovery under the nominal held-out distribution, but that this advantage is distribution-dependent under zero-shot OOD shifts.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} task graph; multi-relation coordination; post-failure recovery; heterogeneous UAV cooperative interception; multi-agent reinforcement learning; limited communication; intermittent sensing
