\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning}

Proximal policy optimization is a widely used policy-gradient method for stable on-policy optimization \cite{schulman2017ppo}. In cooperative multi-agent tasks, MAPPO has been shown to be a strong baseline when implementation details and hyperparameters are handled carefully \cite{yu2022mappo}. Centralized training with decentralized execution is especially relevant for UAV teams, because the critic can use global training information while deployed agents operate from local observations and available communication.

Actor-critic methods for mixed cooperative-competitive tasks also motivate centralized critics and decentralized policies \cite{lowe2017maddpg}. In the present work, MAPPO is used as the learning backbone, while the main contribution is the role-aware multi-relation graph representation used by the actor.

\subsection{Graph Neural Networks for Multi-Agent Systems}

Graph neural networks are natural tools for representing multi-agent interaction topology. Graph attention networks aggregate neighbor information with attention weights and have been widely adopted as a generic relation encoder \cite{velickovic2018gat}. Recent surveys also summarize the growing connection between graph neural networks and multi-agent reinforcement learning \cite{liu2024gnn_marl}.

Recent air-combat studies have also introduced graph encoders into cooperative maneuver decision learning. Graph-convolutional air-combat decision methods model UAVs as graph nodes to improve multi-aircraft coordination \cite{ou2024air_combat_gcn_drl}, and larger-scale graph-based air-combat policies have been studied for multi-aircraft engagements \cite{huo2025graph_air_combat}. These works support the suitability of graph representations for air-combat decision problems, but they mainly emphasize maneuver coordination or large-scale engagement efficiency.

For heterogeneous UAV kill-chain recovery, a single adjacency matrix is not sufficient. The same pair of nodes can be related by sensing, communication, or task-support semantics, and the relevance of each relation changes when a relay node fails. The present work therefore uses a multi-relation graph and preserves a single-graph variant as a controlled baseline.

\subsection{Limited Communication and Intermittent Sensing}

Real UAV teams cannot assume globally available information. Communication links can disappear because of distance, dropout, delay, interference, or node failure. Sensing can also be intermittent due to radar field of view, range, and dropout. Communication-aware MADRL has been systematically studied from perspectives such as communication targets, message content, topology, and learning protocols \cite{zhu2024comm_madrl_survey}. Recent robust communication methods further show that GNN-based message passing can be sensitive to perturbations and noisy links, motivating mechanisms that improve robustness rather than only increasing message capacity \cite{ding2024magi}.

This paper focuses on a complementary question: how to structure role-aware relational reasoning so that a heterogeneous team can recover a kill chain when communication support is disrupted. Instead of learning a generic communication protocol, the proposed actor separates perception, communication, and task-support relations, and conditions message mapping on sender and receiver roles.

\subsection{UAV Air-Combat Decision Learning}

Deep reinforcement learning has become a major approach for autonomous air-combat maneuver decision-making, with recent reviews emphasizing the need for reproducible environments, stable training protocols, and credible comparisons \cite{wang2024air_combat_drl_review}. Many existing studies focus on 1v1 maneuvering, pursuit-evasion, or direct win-rate maximization. The present work narrows the task to strict-sensing relay-failure recovery in a heterogeneous team, where the main question is not only whether the target is eventually engaged, but whether the team restores tracking, communication connectivity, and attack-window availability after losing a key functional node.
