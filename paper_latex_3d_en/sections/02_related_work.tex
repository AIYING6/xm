\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning under Partial Observability}

Proximal policy optimization is a widely used policy-gradient method for stable on-policy training \cite{schulman2017ppo}. In cooperative multi-agent tasks, MAPPO is a strong baseline when implementation details are handled carefully \cite{yu2022mappo}; value-factorization methods such as QMIX address credit assignment for fully cooperative settings \cite{rashid2018qmix}, and heterogeneous-agent trust-region methods (HATRPO/HAPPO) provide monotonic improvement guarantees for heterogeneous agents \cite{kuba2022happo}. Actor-critic methods for mixed cooperative-competitive tasks motivate centralized critics with decentralized policies \cite{lowe2017maddpg}. Centralized training with decentralized execution is especially relevant for UAV teams: the critic can use global training information while deployed agents act from local observations and available communication. In the present work, MAPPO serves as the learning backbone; the contribution is the role-aware multi-relation task-graph representation used by the actor.

\subsection{Graph-Based MARL and Learned Communication}

Graph neural networks are natural tools for representing multi-agent interaction topology. Graph attention networks aggregate neighbor information with attention weights and have been widely adopted as relation encoders \cite{velickovic2018gat}; recent surveys connect graph neural networks and multi-agent reinforcement learning more broadly \cite{liu2024gnn_marl}. Learned-communication methods show how agents can decide what and to whom to communicate: from continuous broadcast protocols \cite{sukhbaatar2016commnet} to attention-based targeted communication \cite{jiang2018i2c, das2019tarmac}. Under communication constraints, graph-information-bottleneck methods learn robust yet efficient message passing \cite{ding2024magi}.

Recent air-combat studies have introduced graph encoders into cooperative maneuver decision learning. Graph-convolutional air-combat methods model UAVs as graph nodes to improve coordination \cite{ou2024air_combat_gcn_drl}, and graph-based air-combat policies have been studied for multi-aircraft engagements \cite{huo2025graph_air_combat}. These works support graph representations for air-combat decisions, but they mainly emphasize maneuver coordination or engagement efficiency.

For heterogeneous kill-chain recovery, a single adjacency matrix is insufficient: the same node pair can be related by sensing, communication, or task-support semantics, and the relevance of each relation changes when a relay node fails. The present work therefore uses a multi-relation task graph and retains a wider single-graph variant as a controlled baseline.

\subsection{Limited Communication and Intermittent Sensing}

Real UAV teams cannot assume globally available information. Links can disappear because of distance, dropout, delay, interference, or node failure, and sensing can be intermittent. Communication-aware MARL has been studied from the perspectives of communication targets, message content, topology, and learning protocols \cite{zhu2024comm_madrl_survey}. Robust-communication methods further show that GNN-based message passing can be sensitive to noisy links, motivating mechanisms that improve robustness rather than only message capacity \cite{ding2024magi}. This paper studies a complementary question: how to structure role-aware relational reasoning so that a heterogeneous team can recover a kill chain when communication support is disrupted.

\subsection{UAV Air-Combat Decision Learning}

Deep reinforcement learning has become a major approach for autonomous air-combat maneuver decision-making \cite{wang2024air_combat_drl_review}. Most studies focus on 1v1 maneuvering, pursuit-evasion, or direct win-rate maximization. The present work narrows the task to strict-sensing relay-failure recovery in a heterogeneous team, where the main question is not only whether the target is eventually engaged, but whether the team restores tracking, communication connectivity, and attack-window availability after losing a key functional node.

\subsection{Failure Recovery and Resilience in Multi-Agent Systems}

Resilience under component failure has been studied in multi-agent control and learning. Recent learning-based work applies hierarchical MARL to dispatch repair resources and restore system functionality after failures \cite{qiu2023resilience}. In multi-robot systems, robust distributed state estimation (e.g., Kimera-Multi \cite{tian2022kimeramulti}) and collaborative exploration systems \cite{zhou2023racer} demonstrate resilience to individual-agent failures in perception and navigation. However, these works typically evaluate terminal success, robustness to perturbations, or reconfiguration ability; they pay limited attention to the \emph{distribution of recovery time} after a critical node or communication failure.

\subsection{Censor-aware Evaluation of Recovery Dynamics}

Evaluating time-to-event outcomes requires accounting for right censoring, i.e., episodes that terminate before the event is observed. The Kaplan--Meier estimator provides a nonparametric survival curve \cite{kaplan1958}, and the restricted mean survival time (RMST) summarizes the survival distribution up to a fixed horizon without relying on a proportional-hazards assumption \cite{royston2011rmst, uno2014rmst}. In the multi-agent recovery setting, a conditional mean recovery time (averaged only over recovered episodes) can overstate differences between methods with different terminal recovery probabilities; a censor-aware RMST analysis avoids this bias. To our knowledge, the censor-aware recovery-time distribution after key-node failure has received little attention in the multi-UAV MARL literature, which is the gap this paper addresses.
