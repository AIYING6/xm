\section{Discussion}

The locked three-seed evidence supports a focused claim: task-graph-driven multi-relation learning improves the \emph{reliability--recovery-speed trade-off} in post-failure coordination recovery under strict intermittent sensing and relay-node communication failure. We report three trade-offs explicitly rather than claiming uniform dominance.

First, Full does not achieve the globally highest final reliability. HAPPO and the wider single-graph baseline reach recovery rates of 1.000 and 0.995, respectively, slightly above Full's $0.971 \pm 0.021$. A censor-aware survival analysis further shows that the full-horizon recovery dynamics are competitive rather than uniformly superior: the primary RMST is $14.47 \pm 3.10$ for EA-RG, close to HAPPO's $14.14 \pm 2.94$ and to the w/o Role-Pair Modulation variant's $13.63 \pm 3.86$, and the per-seed differences against HAPPO are mixed ($+4.64 / -1.00 / -2.67$). The advantage of Full is therefore not a uniformly shorter full-horizon recovery distribution. Instead, it is concentrated in the early post-failure period: at $\tau = 80$, corresponding to the active node-failure interval, EA-RG reduces RMST from $15.51$ to $11.81$ steps relative to MAPPO with all three seeds in the same direction, and the same pattern holds at $\tau = 50$ and $100$. In other words, the method shifts task-level recovery toward the early post-failure window while retaining near-saturated terminal reliability, rather than improving every part of the recovery-time distribution.

Second, computational cost is higher. Full has 117{,}302 parameters, a 12.05 ms joint-decision latency, 242 env-steps/s end-to-end throughput, and the largest training peak memory (71.9 MB) among the compared methods. The method trades additional computational cost for faster task-level recovery; the two notions of efficiency (computational latency vs.\ recovery latency in environment steps) must be kept separate.

Third, the role-pair modulation provides limited independent benefit. Removing it leaves held-out recovery effectively unchanged or slightly higher (0.990 vs.\ 0.971), leaves the primary censor-aware RMST essentially unchanged (13.63 vs.\ 14.47, seed-mixed), and does not improve robustness under strong perturbations in a consistent direction. It is therefore retained only as an auxiliary architectural component, and the paper does not attribute communication compression or robustness gains to it. The strongest mechanism evidence instead concerns Gate Prior: a structured role-pair initialization that raises cross-seed gate correlation from 0.562 to 0.962 and improves worst-seed training stability. Removing the task-support relation degrades held-out recovery (0.971 $\rightarrow$ 0.892) and increases recovery latency (10.8 $\rightarrow$ 15.0), but temporal analysis does not reveal a consistent post-failure activation increase; its benefit is therefore supported empirically rather than by a specific temporal mechanism.

These limitations define the next development path. The current result package is suitable as a strong 3v1 mechanism foundation. To target a higher-tier submission, future work should add carefully controlled scenario-depth experiments, such as more training seeds, an external communication baseline, and unseen-layout / failure generalization, without diluting the current locked evidence chain.
