\section{Discussion}

The locked three-seed evidence supports a focused claim: task-graph-driven multi-relation learning improves the \emph{reliability--recovery-speed trade-off} in post-failure coordination recovery under strict intermittent sensing and relay-node communication failure. We report three trade-offs explicitly rather than claiming uniform dominance.

First, Full does not achieve the globally highest final reliability. HAPPO and the wider single-graph baseline reach recovery rates of 1.000 and 0.995, respectively, slightly above Full's $0.971 \pm 0.021$. A censor-aware survival analysis further shows that the full-horizon recovery dynamics are competitive rather than uniformly superior: the primary RMST is $14.47 \pm 3.10$ for EA-RG, close to HAPPO's $14.14 \pm 2.94$ and to the w/o Role-Pair Modulation variant's $13.63 \pm 3.86$, and the per-seed differences against HAPPO are mixed ($+4.64 / -1.00 / -2.67$). The advantage of Full is therefore not a uniformly shorter full-horizon recovery distribution. Instead, it is concentrated in the early post-failure period: at $\tau = 80$, corresponding to the active node-failure interval, EA-RG reduces RMST from $15.51$ to $11.81$ steps relative to MAPPO with all three seeds in the same direction, and the same pattern holds at $\tau = 50$ and $100$. In other words, the method shifts task-level recovery toward the early post-failure window while retaining near-saturated terminal reliability, rather than improving every part of the recovery-time distribution.

Second, computational cost is higher. Full has 117{,}302 parameters, a 12.05 ms joint-decision latency, 242 env-steps/s end-to-end throughput, and the largest training peak memory (71.9 MB) among the compared methods. The method trades additional computational cost for the earlier post-failure task-chain recovery characterized in RQ2; the two notions of efficiency (computational latency vs.\ recovery latency in environment steps) must be kept separate.

Third, the role-pair modulation provides limited independent benefit. Removing it leaves held-out recovery effectively unchanged or slightly higher (0.990 vs.\ 0.971), leaves the primary censor-aware RMST essentially unchanged (13.63 vs.\ 14.47, seed-mixed), and does not improve robustness under strong perturbations in a consistent direction. It is therefore retained only as an auxiliary architectural component, and the paper does not attribute communication compression or robustness gains to it. The strongest mechanism evidence instead concerns Gate Prior: a structured role-pair initialization that raises cross-seed gate correlation from 0.562 to 0.962 and improves worst-seed training stability. Removing the task-support relation degrades held-out recovery (0.971 $\rightarrow$ 0.892) and increases recovery latency (10.8 $\rightarrow$ 15.0), but temporal analysis does not reveal a consistent post-failure activation increase; its benefit is therefore supported empirically rather than by a specific temporal mechanism.

\subsection{Boundary of the early-recovery advantage under zero-shot OOD shifts}

A pre-specified zero-shot OOD evaluation (RQ7, protocol v1.1) delimits where the early-recovery advantage holds. Four findings define the boundary. First, under the \emph{nominal held-out} distribution the advantage is reproducible: three seeds agree at $\tau=50/80/100$, with a bootstrap interval excluding zero. Second, \emph{geometry shifts} (spacing/rotation, range/bearing) preserve part of the benefit, although more weakly and with one seed reverting. Third, \emph{communication-topology shifts} reverse the comparison: with the longest blue--blue edge pruned, MAPPO attains a higher recovery rate ($0.960$ vs.\ $0.770$) and a lower RMST80, and this reversal is consistent across seeds. Fourth, \emph{maneuver and joint shifts} saturate the recovery endpoint: within $\tau=80$ no method re-establishes the kill chain (recovery $\approx 0$), so all methods sit at the RMST80 ceiling and the comparison is uninformative at that horizon---not because Full and MAPPO behave alike, but because the shift overwhelms every evaluated method.

The combined boundary is therefore: EA-RG provides reproducible early post-failure recovery gains under the nominal held-out distribution, but this advantage is distribution-dependent under zero-shot OOD shifts. Geometry shifts preserve part of the benefit, communication-topology shifts can reverse the comparison, and maneuver shifts can overwhelm all evaluated methods. We interpret this as evidence about \emph{where} the method helps, not as a claim of across-shift generalization. The multi-relation task graph encodes relationships that were learned on the training distribution; under topology pruning that removes the longest blue--blue edge, the learned communication-support structure transfers poorly relative to a simpler policy, while under severe maneuver shifts the recovery endpoint itself becomes inaccessible to all compared methods.

\subsection{Limitations}

We state the main limitations explicitly.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Three independent training seeds.} All cross-method statistics use $n=3$
    independent replications; seed-wise differences are reported in full, but the precision
    of the estimates is limited by this sample size.
    \item \textbf{Fixed 3-UAV/1-target role structure.} The scout--relay--attacker team is
    fixed; scalability to larger teams or multi-target settings is not validated.
    \item \textbf{3DOF dynamics.} Motion is simplified to three degrees of freedom; full
    6DOF aerodynamics, missiles, and radar models are out of scope.
    \item \textbf{No visual semantic perception pipeline.} Target information arrives as
    abstract detection states; the method is not a vision-language perception system.
    \item \textbf{Full-horizon recovery is competitive, not uniformly superior.} The primary
    RMST is lower than MAPPO and the wider single-graph baseline but close to HAPPO
    ($14.47$ vs.\ $14.14$) and to the w/o Role-Pair Modulation variant; the most consistent
    advantage is concentrated in the early post-failure period ($\tau \le 100$).
    \item \textbf{Role-Pair Modulation contributes little independently.} It is retained as
    an auxiliary component; it does not provide communication compression, robustness, or a
    stable full-horizon recovery benefit.
    \item \textbf{Zero-shot OOD generalization is limited.} A pre-specified OOD evaluation
    (protocol v1.1, RQ7) shows that the early-recovery advantage does not transfer uniformly:
    it is partially preserved under geometry shifts, reversed under communication-topology
    shifts, and overwhelmed by maneuver shifts, yielding an overall Gate~C verdict. The
    evaluated shifts are one sample of possible shifts; other unseen distribution changes may
    behave differently, and no re-tuning was performed after inspecting these results.
    \item \textbf{Task-Support benefit is empirical.} Removing it degrades held-out recovery
    and slows recovery, but the internal temporal mechanism remains inconclusive; no
    post-failure activation re-organization is claimed.
\end{enumerate}

These limitations define the next development path. The current result package is suitable
as a strong 3v1 mechanism foundation. To target a higher-tier submission, future work should
add carefully controlled scenario-depth experiments, such as more training seeds, an external
communication baseline, and genuinely unseen-distribution generalization, without diluting
the current locked evidence chain.
