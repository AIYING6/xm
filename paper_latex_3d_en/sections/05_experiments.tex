\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate the proposed method in a 3DOF heterogeneous UAV cooperative-interception environment with three blue agents (scout, relay, attacker) and a target. All methods are trained with three independent random seeds for $977$ PPO updates. Validation and held-out evaluation are strictly separated: validation (base seed 641939) is used \emph{only} for checkpoint selection, and all reported results come from a locked held-out split with base seed 745669, evaluated exactly once. The held-out evaluation covers four formal scenarios (nominal and early relay failure, with and without the delayed-communication configuration); each selected checkpoint is evaluated over 100 episodes per scenario per seed, giving 10{,}800 held-out episodes in total. Robustness evaluation (base seed 946804) covers seven methods $\times$ ten perturbation conditions $\times$ three seeds $\times$ 50 episodes, i.e., 10{,}500 episodes.

An episode is successful if the attacker closes the kill chain within the horizon without collision; episodes with collision are ineligible for success. The recovery metric is defined as the number of failure-exposed episodes in which the post-failure kill chain is re-established, divided by the number of failure-exposed episodes ($\mathrm{recovered\_given\_exposure} / \mathrm{failure\_exposed}$). Recovery latency $t_{\mathrm{rec}}$ is defined only for episodes that successfully recover. Reported values are the mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation (ddof=1) over the three training seeds.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{EA-RG Full}: the proposed task-graph-driven multi-relation method.
    \item \textbf{Internal ablations}: w/o Gate Prior, w/o Task-Support, w/o Role-Pair Gate.
    \item \textbf{Graph baselines}: no-graph, single-graph, and a wider single-graph baseline (a higher-capacity encoder with a larger parameter budget than the single-graph baseline).
    \item \textbf{External MARL baselines}: MAPPO and HAPPO, evaluated with the same held-out protocol.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ1: Does EA-RG achieve competitive held-out task performance?}

Table~\ref{tab:held_out} reports held-out performance. EA-RG reaches a success rate of $0.985 \pm 0.011$ and a recovery rate of $0.971 \pm 0.021$, placing it in the first tier. At the same time, HAPPO ($1.000$) and the wider single-graph baseline ($0.995$) obtain slightly higher final recovery rates, and even the w/o Role-Pair Gate ablation reaches $0.990$. EA-RG therefore achieves near-saturated held-out reliability, although it does not obtain the highest terminal recovery probability among the compared methods.

The temporal metrics tell a different story. EA-RG completes successful tasks in $46.1 \pm 0.4$ steps and re-establishes the kill chain after failure in $10.8 \pm 0.6$ steps, the lowest conditional recovery latency among all methods and the smallest seed-level variance. Because this quantity is defined only for episodes that recover, it motivates a censor-aware analysis of \emph{when} recovery occurs across the full post-failure horizon (RQ2).

\subsection{RQ2: How does EA-RG affect censor-aware post-failure recovery dynamics?}

The conditional recovery latency reported in Table~\ref{tab:held_out} describes only failure-exposed episodes that eventually recover. We therefore denote this quantity as the \emph{conditional mean recovery time among recovered failure-exposed episodes}. Although useful as a descriptive measure, it does not account for episodes in which recovery is not observed before termination. To evaluate the complete post-failure recovery-time distribution, we additionally perform a pre-specified censor-aware survival analysis using Kaplan--Meier estimation and restricted mean survival time (RMST).

The survival analysis uses failure onset as the time origin. A recovery event is defined at the start of the first stable post-failure task-chain closure window, consistent with the recovery clock used in the formal evaluation. Episodes that remain unrecovered until termination are right-censored at their available post-failure observation time. The primary analysis includes the early- and nominal-failure scenarios, for which every method, training seed, and scenario contains exactly 100 failure-exposed episodes. The delayed- and late-failure scenarios are not pooled into the primary survival comparison because many episodes terminate before failure occurs and the resulting exposure rate is method dependent; these scenarios are retained only for terminal-reliability and landmark-sensitivity analyses.

The primary endpoint is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RMST}(\tau)=\int_{0}^{\tau} S(t)\,dt,
\label{eq:rmst}
\end{equation}
where $S(t)=P(T>t)$ is the Kaplan--Meier estimate of the probability that recovery has not yet occurred. The common primary horizon is fixed at $\tau=220$ steps, the largest complete post-failure follow-up shared by the primary scenarios. A lower RMST indicates earlier recovery after accounting for both observed recovery events and right-censored episodes.

Figure~\ref{fig:km_recovery} shows the primary Kaplan--Meier recovery curves, and Table~\ref{tab:rmst} summarizes the RMST at the pre-specified horizons. EA-RG obtains an RMST of $14.47 \pm 3.10$ steps across the three training seeds. The corresponding values are $20.39 \pm 7.72$ for MAPPO, $14.14 \pm 2.94$ for HAPPO, and $16.49 \pm 8.64$ for the wider single-graph baseline. Thus, EA-RG has a lower mean full-horizon RMST than MAPPO and the wider single-graph baseline, but the seed-wise differences are not uniformly favorable. In particular, the paired Full-minus-baseline RMST differences are $(-1.22\,/\,-17.67\,/\,+1.11)$ steps against MAPPO and $(-9.01\,/\,+0.87\,/\,+2.06)$ against the wider single-graph baseline. HAPPO achieves a slightly lower mean RMST than EA-RG ($14.14$ versus $14.47$), with mixed seed-wise differences of $(+4.64\,/\,-1.00\,/\,-2.67)$. We therefore do not interpret the primary RMST results as evidence of uniform full-horizon superiority.

The recovery advantage becomes more consistent during the early post-failure period. The pre-specified RMST sensitivity analysis evaluates $\tau = 50$, $80$, and $100$ steps in addition to the primary horizon. At $\tau = 80$, which corresponds to the duration for which the failed node remains inactive, EA-RG achieves an RMST of $11.81$ steps compared with $15.51$ for MAPPO. The paired Full-minus-MAPPO differences are $(-2.64)$, $(-7.27)$, and $(-1.21)$ steps for the three training seeds, respectively. A paired hierarchical bootstrap gives a 95\% interval of $[-7.16, -1.05]$ steps. The same direction of improvement is observed for all three seeds at both $\tau = 50$ and $\tau = 100$. This consistency indicates that the principal temporal benefit of EA-RG relative to MAPPO is concentrated in the early recovery period, particularly while the node failure remains active.

These censor-aware results refine the interpretation obtained from the conditional recovery-time metric. EA-RG retains the lowest conditional mean recovery time among the compared methods, $10.8 \pm 0.6$ steps, but this quantity alone overstates the difference from methods with higher terminal recovery probability. The Kaplan--Meier and RMST analyses show instead that EA-RG provides a reproducible early-recovery advantage over MAPPO while remaining competitive, rather than uniformly superior, over the full observation horizon against HAPPO and the wider single-graph baseline. The main contribution is therefore a shift toward earlier post-failure task-chain restoration rather than universal dominance of the complete recovery-time distribution.

\subsection{RQ3: Which components contribute to performance?}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports the internal ablations in the order of their held-out effect.

\input{tables/table2_ablation.tex}

\textbf{Gate Prior is the strongest internal component.} Removing it causes the largest reliability degradation among the tested components: recovery drops from $0.971$ to $0.772 \pm 0.244$, and recovery latency increases from $10.8$ to $15.3 \pm 2.5$ steps, with a marked rise in seed-level heterogeneity.

\textbf{Task-Support provides a clear empirical contribution.} Removing it lowers recovery from $0.971$ to $0.892 \pm 0.160$ and increases recovery latency from $10.8$ to $15.0 \pm 4.5$ steps. We deliberately do not interpret this as evidence of post-failure relational re-organization: the temporal analysis in the Discussion shows that Task-Support activation \emph{decreases} rather than increases immediately after failure (from 0.141 in the pre-failure window to 0.092 in the early post-failure window), with no consistent pre-recovery rebound. Its benefit is therefore supported empirically.

\textbf{The static Role-Pair Modulation shows limited independent benefit.} Removing it leaves recovery effectively unchanged or slightly higher ($0.990$ vs.\ $0.971$) and leaves the primary censor-aware RMST essentially unchanged ($13.63 \pm 3.86$ vs.\ $14.47 \pm 3.10$, with mixed per-seed differences). Consistent with the robustness and efficiency evidence below, we do not regard the Role-Pair Modulation as an independently validated core component; it is retained as an auxiliary architectural component.

\subsection{RQ4: How robust is EA-RG under communication and failure perturbations?}

Figure~\ref{fig:robustness} shows recovery degradation relative to the R00 (nominal) condition across ten perturbation conditions, grouped into four families: communication dropout (R01--R02), message delay (R03--R04), failure timing / failed role (R05--R08), and joint stress (R09). Conditions with insufficient failure exposure were marked unstable and excluded from deterministic mechanism conclusions (these are R06 and R08 in the table).

EA-RG remains competitive under severe perturbations, but it does not dominate every baseline or ablation. The important preregistered question is whether the Role-Pair Gate contributes robustness. The preregistered verdict cells are: R02 (strong communication dropout): Full $0.940$ vs.\ w/o Role-Pair Gate $1.000$; R04 (long delay): Full $1.000$ vs.\ w/o Role-Pair Gate $0.993$; R09 (joint stress): Full $0.927$ vs.\ w/o Role-Pair Gate $0.987$. The preregistered robustness criterion therefore does not support the Role-Pair Gate as an independent robustness mechanism.

\subsection{RQ5: What is the computational cost of earlier task-chain recovery?}

Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} reports the locked efficiency profiling. EA-RG is computationally heavier than MAPPO, HAPPO, and the wider single-graph baseline: $117{,}302$ parameters, a $12.05$\,ms joint-decision latency, $83$ joint decisions/s, $242$ env-steps/s end-to-end throughput, and the largest training peak memory ($71.9$\,MB). The benefit therefore lies in task-level recovery efficiency rather than per-forward computational efficiency: the method trades additional computational cost for the earlier post-failure task-chain recovery characterized in RQ2. These two notions of efficiency must be kept separate.

We also report the communication cost precisely. The graph edge-feature dimension ($17$) describes \emph{graph computation} only. The actual transmitted message consists of $7$ continuous scalars (positions, velocities, and a confidence), integer metadata, and a variable-length path. Crucially, the physical communication statistics of EA-RG and its w/o Role-Pair Gate ablation are identical (fixed shared trajectory): the Role-Pair Gate does not provide bandwidth compression or message pruning.

\subsection{RQ6: Why does Gate Prior improve stability?}

Figure~\ref{fig:gate_prior} shows the locked parameter-trajectory analysis. The strongest results are the following. First, the cross-seed gate correlation is $0.962$ with the prior versus $0.562$ without it: the prior starts all seeds from a shared structured initialization that remains consistent across seeds. Second, the training success AUC improves from $0.396$ to $0.545$, and the first evaluation point reaching success $\geq 0.9$ is attained at updates $\{200, 200, 600\}$ across seeds with the prior, versus $\{600, 500, \text{not reached}\}$ without it, matching the much weaker worst-seed behavior observed without the prior. Third, gate logits drift by less than $\approx 0.02$ after update 100, and the gate saturation fraction is zero in both configurations: the learned modulation never collapses to hard on/off states.

We therefore conclude that Gate Prior does not act by driving the learned modulation toward saturated on/off states. Instead, it provides a structured initialization that remains largely preserved during training and substantially increases cross-seed consistency. This interpretation is consistent with, but does not by itself prove, a causal link to the worst-seed improvements.

\subsection{RQ7: Does the early-recovery advantage transfer to zero-shot out-of-distribution shifts?}

The held-out, robustness, and efficiency results characterize performance within the training distribution. To test whether the early-recovery advantage persists under genuinely unseen distribution shifts, we run a pre-specified zero-shot OOD evaluation (protocol v1.1) that reuses the locked held-out checkpoints and a new evaluation seed schedule (base seed 1208607) over seven OOD cells: two geometry shifts (spacing $\times 1.20$ with a $+20^{\circ}$ rotation, G1; target range $\times 1.40$ with bearing offset $+25^{\circ}$, G2), two target-maneuver shifts (weaving, M1; break-turn, M2), two communication-topology shifts (longest blue--blue edge pruned symmetrically, C1; pruned as a directed lower-to-higher-$y$ edge, C2), and a joint cell (J1) combining G1, M1, and C1. Each method $\times$ training seed $\times$ cell is evaluated zero-shot over 100 episodes (8400 episodes total), with the same failure onset (step 25), failure duration (80), horizon (260), and recovery clock as the held-out survival analysis. No retraining, fine-tuning, checkpoint reselection, or parameter adjustment was performed after inspecting these results.

Table~\ref{tab:ood} reports the primary OOD estimand: the per-seed seven-cell equal-weight aggregate of Full-minus-MAPPO RMST80 differences,
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{OOD}_s=\frac{1}{7}\sum_{c}\left(\mathrm{RMST80}_{\mathrm{Full},s,c}-\mathrm{RMST80}_{\mathrm{MAPPO},s,c}\right).
\label{eq:delta_ood}
\end{equation}
The seed-level aggregates are $-2.420$, $+1.543$, and $+8.571$ steps, giving a mean of $+2.565 \pm 5.567$ steps. A hierarchical paired bootstrap ($B=10{,}000$, RNG 20260807) yields a 95\% interval of $[-2.362,\ +8.435]$ with $P(\Delta<0)=0.175$. The three training seeds do not agree in direction (s0 favors Full; s1 and s2 favor MAPPO), and the aggregate is not significantly different from zero.

The family-level decomposition explains the aggregate. \textbf{Geometry shifts preserve part of the benefit}: the G1/G2 family shows a small Full advantage ($-1.080$ steps on average). \textbf{Communication-topology shifts reverse the comparison}: under C1/C2, Full has a higher RMST80 ($32.7$ vs.\ $22.6$ steps) and a lower recovery rate ($0.770$ vs.\ $0.960$), so MAPPO is clearly favored ($+10.057$ family effect), and this reversal is directionally consistent across all three seeds. \textbf{Maneuver and Joint shifts produce RMST80 early-window saturation}: in M1/M2/J1, essentially no recovery event occurs within the $\tau=80$ early window for any compared method (recovery largely fails; the only exception is a single seed of M2 for MAPPO with 14 late recoveries all after step 100, hence outside the $\tau=80$ window), so all methods reach the RMST80 ceiling of $80$ and the per-cell differences are identically zero. This saturation is a genuine property of these cells rather than a methodological artifact: the maneuver shifts are severe enough that post-failure kill-chain recovery fails for all evaluated methods, including HAPPO and the wider single-graph baseline.

We therefore report \textbf{Gate C}: the early-recovery advantage is distribution-dependent. It is reproducible under the nominal held-out distribution, partially preserved under geometry shifts, reversed by communication-topology shifts, and overwhelmed by maneuver and joint shifts. We deliberately do not drop the saturated cells from the estimand, do not re-tune $\tau$, and do not re-weight the families: the seven-cell equal-weight aggregate above is the frozen primary OOD summary, and the boundary it reveals is treated as the scientific finding.
