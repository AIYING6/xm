\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Protocol}

We evaluate the proposed method in a 3DOF heterogeneous cooperative interception task with strict intermittent target sensing and temporary relay-node communication failure. The decentralized actors do not receive global target-state fallback. Target information is available only through direct sensing or valid communication messages, and stale or low-confidence target caches cannot close the kill chain. Delayed communication follows post-step timing, and the relay-failure window is applied consistently during evaluation.

The current paper-facing result package uses a fixed-budget checkpoint rule. Each method is trained for 60 safety-continuation PPO updates from the same source-policy family and is evaluated at the fixed checkpoint \texttt{actor\_critic\_update\_0060.pt}. No validation-based checkpoint selection is used in this fixed-budget package. The test scenario is \texttt{dropout030\_relay\_failure}. Each method is evaluated with five training seeds and 100 matched test episodes per seed.

The primary metrics are post-failure chain recovery rate, tracking rate during failure, communication connectivity during failure, chain-closure rate during failure, timeout rate, and collision rate. Statistical intervals use a hierarchical bootstrap: training seeds are resampled first, and matched episodes are then resampled within each seed.

\subsection{Main Comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:gate1-safety-fx60-main} reports the fixed-update-60 main comparison. The full multi-relation method achieves an 88.6\% post-failure recovery rate, compared with 53.2\% for the single-graph baseline and 21.8\% for the no-graph MAPPO-style baseline. The full method also improves failure-window tracking from 47.5\% to 77.6\% relative to the single-graph baseline and from 14.8\% to 77.6\% relative to the no-graph baseline.

\input{../results/gate1_safety_fx60_paper_tables/main_results_latex.tex}

The seed-aware bootstrap indicates that the recovery improvement over the single-graph baseline is 35.4 percentage points, with a 95\% confidence interval of [1.2, 73.0] percentage points. Against the no-graph baseline, the recovery improvement is 66.8 percentage points, with a 95\% confidence interval of [28.6, 93.8] percentage points. The full method maintains zero test collisions in this fixed-budget evaluation, while the single-graph baseline has a 2.8\% mean collision rate.

These results show that under strict information constraints, explicit multi-relation role-graph reasoning improves the ability to recover a cooperative kill chain after a critical communication-node failure.

\subsection{Failure-Timing Generalization}

Table~\ref{tab:gate1-safety-fx60-timing} evaluates whether the fixed-update-60 policies depend on the nominal relay-failure onset time. The same checkpoints are evaluated without retraining under an earlier relay-failure onset. The early-failure setting remains discriminative: the full multi-relation method recovers the kill chain in 88.2\% of episodes, compared with 46.6\% for the single-graph baseline and 23.2\% for the no-graph baseline. The seed-aware full-minus-single recovery delta is 41.6 percentage points with a 95\% confidence interval of [4.4, 78.6] percentage points.

\input{../results/gate1_safety_fx60_failure_timing_generalization_formal_merged/timing_generalization_latex.tex}

Delayed and late relay-failure onsets are not used as primary evidence because many successful episodes terminate before the delayed failure window contributes valid post-failure measurements. Therefore, the timing result supports robustness to earlier-than-nominal relay loss, not arbitrary failure-time robustness.

\subsection{Mechanism Ablations}

Table~\ref{tab:gate1-safety-fx60-ablation} evaluates two mechanism ablations of the full multi-relation model.

\input{../results/gate1_safety_fx60_paper_tables/ablation_results_latex.tex}

Removing role-pair-conditioned message gating reduces recovery from 88.6\% to 64.8\%. The seed-aware recovery-rate delta is 23.8 percentage points, with a 95\% confidence interval of [2.8, 59.2] percentage points. This ablation also increases the timeout rate from 11.4\% to 35.2\% and reduces failure-window tracking from 77.6\% to 60.7\%. Because both the full and ablated variants have zero collision rate, the degradation is not explained by unsafe flight termination. This supports role-pair-conditioned message gating as the cleanest current mechanism contribution.

Removing task-support relations also reduces mean recovery from 88.6\% to 64.8\%, but its seed-aware recovery interval crosses zero. Therefore, the task-support relation is reported as supportive mechanism evidence rather than as a statistically decisive result.

\subsection{Failure-Aligned Mechanism Analysis}

Figure~\ref{fig:gate1_safety_fx60_mechanism} shows failure-aligned tracking, connectivity, chain-closure, and recovery-CDF curves. All curves are aligned to the relay-failure start time and aggregated over the matched fixed-budget test set. After relay failure, connectivity drops sharply for all methods, indicating that the full method's advantage is not simply caused by maintaining a fully connected communication graph. Instead, the full method preserves higher target tracking and accumulates recovery events earlier and more consistently after the failure.

\subsection{Model Size and Inference Cost}

Table~\ref{tab:gate1-safety-fx60-model-cost} reports model-size and actor-inference-cost diagnostics for the compared graph encoders. The full multi-relation actor is larger than the single-graph baseline because it maintains separate perception, communication, and task-support channels and role-pair-conditioned message gates. This additional cost is modest in absolute CPU actor-forward latency for the present 3v1 setting, but it should be reported as part of the baseline fairness analysis rather than hidden behind the recovery-rate improvement.

\subsection{Current Limitations}

This fixed-budget package focuses on graph and message mechanisms under a 3v1 strict-sensing relay-failure task. It does not by itself prove that the topology curriculum is the sole source of the performance gain; the no-curriculum ablation is deferred. The current evidence also remains a 3DOF mechanism study rather than a full 4v2 red-blue system with online missile, radar, and 6DOF dynamics. These extensions are treated as future scenario-depth work rather than as claims of the present result.
