\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate the proposed method in a 3DOF heterogeneous UAV semantic-search environment with three blue agents (scout, relay, attacker) and a target. All methods are trained with three independent random seeds for $977$ PPO updates. Validation and held-out evaluation are strictly separated: validation (base seed 641939) is used \emph{only} for checkpoint selection, and all reported results come from a locked held-out split with base seed 745669, evaluated exactly once. The held-out evaluation covers four formal scenarios (nominal and early relay failure, with and without the delayed-communication configuration); each selected checkpoint is evaluated over 100 episodes per scenario per seed, giving 10{,}800 held-out episodes in total. Robustness evaluation (base seed 946804) covers seven methods $\times$ ten perturbation conditions $\times$ three seeds $\times$ 50 episodes, i.e., 10{,}500 episodes.

An episode is successful if the attacker closes the kill chain within the horizon without collision; episodes with collision are ineligible for success. The recovery metric is defined as the number of failure-exposed episodes in which the post-failure kill chain is re-established, divided by the number of failure-exposed episodes ($\mathrm{recovered\_given\_exposure} / \mathrm{failure\_exposed}$). Recovery latency $t_{\mathrm{rec}}$ is defined only for episodes that successfully recover. Reported values are the mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation (ddof=1) over the three training seeds.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{EA-RG Full}: the proposed task-graph-driven multi-relation method.
    \item \textbf{Internal ablations}: w/o Gate Prior, w/o Task-Support, w/o Role-Pair Gate.
    \item \textbf{Graph baselines}: no-graph, single-graph, and a parameter-matched graph baseline (a multi-relation encoder whose parameter count is matched to EA-RG Full; ``matched'' refers to matching the parameter budget).
    \item \textbf{External MARL baselines}: MAPPO and HAPPO, evaluated with the same held-out protocol.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ1: Does EA-RG achieve competitive held-out task performance?}

Table~\ref{tab:held_out} reports held-out performance. EA-RG reaches a success rate of $0.985 \pm 0.011$ and a recovery rate of $0.971 \pm 0.021$, placing it in the first tier. At the same time, HAPPO ($1.000$) and the parameter-matched graph baseline ($0.995$) obtain slightly higher final recovery rates, and even the w/o Role-Pair Gate ablation reaches $0.990$. EA-RG therefore achieves near-saturated held-out reliability, although it does not obtain the highest terminal recovery probability among the compared methods.

The temporal metrics tell a different story. EA-RG completes successful tasks in $46.1 \pm 0.4$ steps and re-establishes the kill chain after failure in $10.8 \pm 0.6$ steps, the lowest recovery latency among all methods and the smallest seed-level variance. These results suggest that the primary advantage of EA-RG is not a higher terminal success probability, but a substantially faster return to effective coordination after failure. This motivates RQ2.

\subsection{RQ2: Does EA-RG accelerate post-failure recovery?}

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto_recovery} plots the reliability--recovery-speed trade-off: each method is a point with recovery rate on the $x$-axis (higher is better) and recovery latency on the $y$-axis (lower is better); error bars are sample standard deviations over the three training seeds. Methods closer to the lower-right are more favorable.

EA-RG occupies the favorable region: near-saturated recovery reliability combined with the lowest recovery latency. Concretely, relative to the locked three-seed means:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Comparison & Recovery & Recovery-time reduction \\
\midrule
EA-RG vs.\ MAPPO & $0.971$ vs.\ $0.947$ & $\approx 38\%$ shorter \\
EA-RG vs.\ HAPPO & $0.971$ vs.\ $1.000$ & $\approx 34\%$ shorter \\
EA-RG vs.\ param-matched & $0.971$ vs.\ $0.995$ & $\approx 59\%$ shorter \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

We emphasize that these are recovery-time reductions, not superiority claims over the competitors' reliability: EA-RG reduces recovery time by approximately 34\% relative to HAPPO while retaining a recovery probability of $0.971$ versus $1.000$. The reduction is especially large against the parameter-matched graph baseline ($26.2 \pm 23.6$ vs.\ $10.8 \pm 0.6$ steps); EA-RG also exhibits substantially lower cross-seed variability in recovery latency (sample SD $0.6$ vs.\ $2.5$--$23.6$). We report these differences descriptively at $n=3$ and do not apply significance tests. The success--completion-time Pareto is provided in Supplementary Fig.~S1.

\subsection{RQ3: Which components contribute to performance?}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports the internal ablations in the order of their held-out effect.

\textbf{Gate Prior is the strongest internal component.} Removing it causes the largest reliability degradation among the tested components: recovery drops from $0.971$ to $0.772 \pm 0.244$, and recovery latency increases from $10.8$ to $15.3 \pm 2.5$ steps, with a marked rise in seed-level heterogeneity.

\textbf{Task-Support provides a clear empirical contribution.} Removing it lowers recovery from $0.971$ to $0.892 \pm 0.160$ and increases recovery latency from $10.8$ to $15.0 \pm 4.5$ steps. We deliberately do not interpret this as evidence of post-failure relational re-organization: the temporal analysis in the Discussion shows that Task-Support activation \emph{decreases} rather than increases immediately after failure (from 0.141 in the pre-failure window to 0.092 in the early post-failure window), with no consistent pre-recovery rebound. Its benefit is therefore supported empirically.

\textbf{The static Role-Pair Gate shows no consistent independent benefit.} Removing it leaves recovery effectively unchanged or slightly higher ($0.990$ vs.\ $0.971$). Consistent with the robustness and efficiency evidence below, we do not regard the Role-Pair Gate as an independently validated core component; it is retained as an auxiliary architectural component.

\subsection{RQ4: How robust is EA-RG under communication and failure perturbations?}

Figure~\ref{fig:robustness} shows recovery degradation relative to the R00 (nominal) condition across ten perturbation conditions, grouped into four families: communication dropout (R01--R02), message delay (R03--R04), failure timing / failed role (R05--R08), and joint stress (R09). Conditions with insufficient failure exposure were marked unstable and excluded from deterministic mechanism conclusions (these are R06 and R08 in the table).

EA-RG remains competitive under severe perturbations, but it does not dominate every baseline or ablation. The important preregistered question is whether the Role-Pair Gate contributes robustness. The preregistered verdict cells are: R02 (strong communication dropout): Full $0.940$ vs.\ w/o Role-Pair Gate $1.000$; R04 (long delay): Full $1.000$ vs.\ w/o Role-Pair Gate $0.993$; R09 (joint stress): Full $0.927$ vs.\ w/o Role-Pair Gate $0.987$. The preregistered robustness criterion therefore does not support the Role-Pair Gate as an independent robustness mechanism.

\subsection{RQ5: What is the computational cost of faster recovery?}

Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} reports the locked efficiency profiling. EA-RG is computationally heavier than MAPPO, HAPPO, and the parameter-matched graph baseline: $117{,}302$ parameters, a $12.05$\,ms joint-decision latency, $83$ joint decisions/s, $242$ env-steps/s end-to-end throughput, and the largest training peak memory ($71.9$\,MB). The benefit therefore lies in task-level recovery efficiency rather than per-forward computational efficiency: the method trades additional computational cost for faster post-failure coordination recovery. These two notions of efficiency must be kept separate.

We also report the communication cost precisely. The graph edge-feature dimension ($17$) describes \emph{graph computation} only. The actual transmitted message consists of $7$ continuous scalars (positions, velocities, and a confidence), integer metadata, and a variable-length path. Crucially, the physical communication statistics of EA-RG and its w/o Role-Pair Gate ablation are identical (fixed shared trajectory): the Role-Pair Gate does not provide bandwidth compression or message pruning.

\subsection{RQ6: Why does Gate Prior improve stability?}

Figure~\ref{fig:gate_prior} shows the locked parameter-trajectory analysis. The strongest results are the following. First, the cross-seed gate correlation is $0.962$ with the prior versus $0.562$ without it: the prior starts all seeds from a shared structured initialization that remains consistent across seeds. Second, the training success AUC improves from $0.396$ to $0.545$, and the first evaluation point reaching success $\geq 0.9$ is attained at updates $\{200, 200, 600\}$ across seeds with the prior, versus $\{600, 500, \text{not reached}\}$ without it, matching the much weaker worst-seed behavior observed without the prior. Third, gate logits drift by less than $\approx 0.02$ after update 100, and the gate saturation fraction is zero in both configurations: the learned modulation never collapses to hard on/off states.

We therefore conclude that Gate Prior does not act by driving the learned modulation toward saturated on/off states. Instead, it provides a structured initialization that remains largely preserved during training and substantially increases cross-seed consistency. This interpretation is consistent with, but does not by itself prove, a causal link to the worst-seed improvements.
