\section{Method}

\subsection{Overview}

EA-RG-MAPPO-S combines a MAPPO-style training objective with a multi-relation role graph actor. The critic uses centralized training information, while the actor receives local observations and graph observations. The policy is executed in a decentralized manner.

The graph representation contains UAV and target nodes. Node roles distinguish scout, relay, attacker, and target entities. Edge features encode relative distance, relative velocity, altitude difference, relative bearing, communication reachability, message age, information confidence, sensing source, and current kill-chain stage.

\subsection{Multi-Relation Role Graph}

The method constructs separate perception, communication, and task-support adjacency matrices. Perception relations indicate which platform can directly detect or track the target. Communication relations indicate which platforms can exchange information. Task-support relations indicate which platform is currently supporting another platform's kill-chain function, such as scout-to-attacker target-information support or relay-to-attacker communication reconstruction.

Role-pair-conditioned message propagation is used so that messages can depend on sender role, receiver role, and relation type. This is the key distinction from a single homogeneous graph encoder.

Let $\mathcal{R}=\{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{comm},\mathrm{sup}\}$ denote the perception, communication, and task-support relation set. For receiver node $i$ and sender node $j$, relation $r$ has adjacency $A^r_{ij}$ and edge feature $e^r_{ij}$. The message passed from $j$ to $i$ is
\begin{equation}
    m^r_{ij} = A^r_{ij} \, g_{r,\rho_i,\rho_j}\left(h_i, h_j, e^r_{ij}\right),
\end{equation}
where $h_i$ and $h_j$ are node embeddings, $\rho_i$ and $\rho_j$ are receiver and sender roles, and $g_{r,\rho_i,\rho_j}$ is the role-pair-conditioned message map. The receiver update aggregates relation-specific messages as
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{h}_i = \phi\left(h_i, \sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\sum_{j} \alpha^r_{ij} m^r_{ij}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\alpha^r_{ij}$ is an edge-aware attention weight. The single-graph baseline replaces $\mathcal{R}$ with one merged adjacency and shared message parameters.

\subsection{Training Protocol}

The training procedure first learns the basic straight-target interception task, then performs fixed-budget safety-continuation training under randomized communication range, dropout, message delay, radar dropout, and temporary blue-node communication failure. Demonstration pretraining, reward shaping, and topology randomization are used as training aids rather than as isolated claimed innovations. The paper-facing comparison therefore emphasizes the graph/message architecture and its mechanism ablations, while a no-curriculum comparison is left for future training-protocol analysis.

The policy is optimized with a MAPPO-style clipped objective under centralized training and decentralized execution:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PPO}} =
    \mathbb{E}\left[
        \min\left(
            r_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t,
            \mathrm{clip}(r_t(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_t
        \right)
    \right],
\end{equation}
where $r_t(\theta)$ is the policy probability ratio and $\hat{A}_t$ is the advantage estimate from the centralized critic. The actor receives decentralized observations and communication-feasible graph information at execution time.
