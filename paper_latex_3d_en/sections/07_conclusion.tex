\section{Conclusion}

This paper presents EA-RG-MAPPO-S, a task-graph-driven multi-relation reinforcement learning method for heterogeneous UAV kill-chain recovery under limited communication and intermittent sensing. The method encodes task-dependency, communication availability, and state-dependent relations into a multi-relation graph and processes them with state-dependent, edge-feature-modulated attention under centralized training and decentralized execution; a structured role-pair initialization prior (Gate Prior) stabilizes role-structured optimization, and a static role-pair modulation is retained as an auxiliary component.

In a locked three-seed held-out evaluation (10{,}800 episodes, strict-sensing relay-failure setting), the method maintains near-saturated recovery reliability ($0.971 \pm 0.021$) while reducing post-failure recovery latency by approximately 38\%, 34\%, and 59\% relative to MAPPO, HAPPO, and a parameter-matched graph baseline (10.8 vs.\ 17.4 / 16.3 / 26.2 steps), with the smallest seed-level variance. Ablations and parameter-trajectory analysis provide strong evidence that Gate Prior improves cross-seed optimization stability (cross-seed gate correlation 0.962 vs.\ 0.562) and worst-seed behavior. Removing the task-support relation degrades held-out recovery and slows recovery, indicating an empirical contribution whose exact temporal mechanism remains inconclusive. The role-pair modulation shows no consistent independent benefit and is therefore not presented as a core contribution.

The current result should be viewed as a 3DOF mechanism foundation rather than a complete operational air-combat system. Future work will extend the evidence chain toward stronger scenario-depth tests, including more training seeds, an external communication baseline, and unseen-layout or failure generalization, with higher-fidelity missile and radar models.
